3 · Moon phases

Like the extension (moon.js paintMoon), the clear-night icon should be the actual moon: a lit limb plus an elliptical terminator whose width follows cos(2π·phase), over a faint earthshine disc so even new moon never vanishes. All lit faces share the neutral light-gray gradient, gray at the base. Eight named phases; in the app the same two arcs render any date's exact phase, and partly-night swaps its crescent for tonight's shape behind the cloud. Stars twinkle around every phase at 44px (the 16px cells are the unanimated stills), placed well clear of the disc — past the painter's radius + 1.4 keep-out — as painter.js moonBody seeds them — so this set ports straight to the shell extension, whose painter already owns the star field and twinkle clock.

4 · Animated, like the extension

The extension's glyphs are alive: sun rays turn once in ~45s, stars twinkle, clouds drift against each other, teardrops fall and fade in under the cloud, flakes sway and rotate, fog bands slide, wind streaks travel, and the storm bolt flashes and rests. The same loops in CSS (wrappers animate, so positioned parts keep their places). 5 · Every condition the app can show

All 28 WMO codes in weather.js (WMO), each with its scene and precipitation-intensity tier (CODE_PRECIP_INTENSITY, dots), plus the derived overrides and the unknown-code fallback. Variants within a scene — drizzle vs rain, showers vs steady rain, light vs heavy — are drawn by swapping precip parts on the shared cloud, so coverage stays systematic rather than 28 one-off drawings.

8 · Rules if this ports to the app

  • One grid: every mark lives on the 24-unit box; the cloud is a single shared path, precip marks hang from a common baseline.
  • Severity by shade: cloud gradients step down partly → overcast → rain → storm, so a week strip scans before it reads.
  • Borrowed tokens: sun/bolt amber is the chart token (#f5a524); water uses the extension's judged inks — deep navy #0a4a96–#1a61cc by day, aqua #61d1ff by night — because the chart blue fails contrast on bright sky.
  • Night is a language, not a filter: crescent replaces the disc; clouds drop one shade step; precip colour stays.
  • Occlusion is honest: sun and moon always draw complete underneath; clouds cover what they cover. (Extension puff and hybrid cloud formations were explored and rejected — the mock dome stays.)
  • Port path: redraw these paths in glyph.js as Cairo vector ops (static, cached raster) — the animated sky stays the painter's job, icons stay crisp and cheap.
